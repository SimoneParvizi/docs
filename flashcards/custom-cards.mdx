Every deck in Blue Accademy supports custom cards alongside the pre-seeded content. You can add a card to any deck at any time, and editing an existing card is just as easy — you can do it right in the middle of a study session without losing your place. Custom cards go through the same spaced-repetition scheduler as every other card, so your new content is treated exactly like the rest of your deck.

## Adding a card to a deck

Click the **+ icon** on the deck row in the deck list to open the Create Card panel for that deck. The panel has three fields:

<ParamField body="Front" required>
  The question or prompt shown on the unflipped side of the card. This is what you see before you test your recall. **Required.**
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Back" required>
  The answer or explanation revealed when you flip the card. **Required.**
</ParamField>

<ParamField body="Code example" optional>
  A code snippet displayed below the answer text when the card is flipped. Shown in a monospace block. Useful for `kubectl` commands, YAML snippets, Dockerfile lines, or Pulumi program examples. **Optional.**
</ParamField>

Fill in at least the Front and Back fields, then click **Add Card**. The card is immediately added to the deck and will appear in your next study session when the scheduler determines it is due.

## Card field formatting

Both the Front and Back fields support a small subset of inline Markdown:

- `**bold text**` renders as **bold text**
- `` `inline code` `` renders as styled inline code

The Code example field is plain text only and is always displayed in a fixed-width monospace block. You do not need to wrap it in backticks or fences.

## Editing a card inline during study

You do not need to leave a study session to fix a typo or improve a card. While studying, click the **pencil icon** (✎) in the top-right corner of the card. The card transitions to an inline editor with three text areas for Front, Back, and Code example. Make your changes, then click **Save**. The updated content is visible immediately — the next time the card is shown, it reflects your edit.

The card's spaced-repetition progress (scheduling state, interval, ease factor, and review history) is fully preserved when you edit its content. Editing a card does not reset or alter when it is next due.

## Editing a card from the deck view

You can also edit any card outside of a study session. Open the deck, locate the card you want to change, and click its edit button to open the same inline editor described above.

<Tip>
  Use the Code example field to attach a reference command or YAML snippet to any card. It appears after the answer text when you flip the card, making it easy to pair a concept question with a concrete example — for instance, pairing "What does `kubectl rollout undo` do?" with the exact command syntax.
</Tip>
