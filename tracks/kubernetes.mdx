Kubernetes is a large surface area, and studying it without structure can leave you with scattered knowledge that is hard to apply under pressure. Blue Accademy's Kubernetes track breaks the platform into four focused decks that take you from fundamental workload primitives all the way through cluster internals and day-two operations. Whether you are preparing for the CKA exam, onboarding onto a new cluster, or reinforcing your production troubleshooting skills, these decks give you a complete picture in a logical order.

## Seed Decks

The Kubernetes track ships with the following four pre-seeded decks:

### 1. K8s Core Concepts

Pods, Deployments, ReplicaSets, DaemonSets, Jobs, CronJobs, and workload fundamentals.

This deck is your entry point into Kubernetes. You'll learn how the scheduler places Pods, how Deployments manage rolling updates and rollbacks, how DaemonSets guarantee one Pod per node, and how Jobs and CronJobs handle batch and scheduled work. It also covers labels, selectors, annotations, and Namespaces — the organizational building blocks every other resource depends on.

### 2. K8s Networking & Services

ClusterIP, NodePort, Ingress, NetworkPolicy, DNS, CNI, headless services.

Services are the Kubernetes mechanism for stable, discoverable endpoints. This deck teaches you how ClusterIP, NodePort, and Ingress expose workloads at different scopes, how CoreDNS resolves service names inside the cluster, how NetworkPolicy restricts traffic at the Pod level, and when to reach for a headless Service to give StatefulSet Pods individually addressable DNS names.

### 3. K8s Config, Storage & Security

ConfigMaps, Secrets, PVs, PVCs, StorageClasses, RBAC, ServiceAccounts, PSS.

Applications need configuration, persistent data, and controlled API access. This deck covers the full lifecycle of ConfigMaps and Secrets (including live volume updates versus environment-variable injection), PersistentVolumes and PersistentVolumeClaims, dynamic provisioning via StorageClasses, and the RBAC model — Roles, ClusterRoles, RoleBindings — tied to ServiceAccounts. Pod Security Standards are also addressed so you understand how admission control enforces container privilege policies.

### 4. K8s Operations & Debugging

HPA, VPA, scheduling, troubleshooting, CRDs, operators, Helm, cluster internals.

This deck focuses on running Kubernetes reliably in production. You'll practice configuring Horizontal Pod Autoscalers, applying taints and tolerations, using node affinity, interpreting `kubectl top` and `kubectl get events`, diagnosing CrashLoopBackOff, managing Helm releases, and understanding the etcd and kube-scheduler internals that underpin the entire control plane.

## Recommended Study Order

<Note>
  Work through the decks in sequence: **Core Concepts → Networking & Services → Config, Storage & Security → Operations & Debugging**. Each deck assumes familiarity with the concepts from the deck before it. For example, understanding RBAC (deck 3) is much easier once you know ServiceAccounts (deck 3) and how Pods communicate (deck 2).
</Note>

1. **K8s Core Concepts** — Build a solid mental model of workload primitives before anything else.
2. **K8s Networking & Services** — Once you know how Pods run, learn how they talk to each other and to the outside world.
3. **K8s Config, Storage & Security** — Add configuration, persistence, and access control to your workloads.
4. **K8s Operations & Debugging** — Bring everything together with autoscaling, scheduling control, and real-world troubleshooting.
